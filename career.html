career

The messy timeline behind the neat version.

Most of this doesn't read as a straight line. Polytechnic instead of junior college, a startup, a hackathon, a few internships in different countries, and a fair amount of not knowing what came next.

2020 · after O-levels

Chose polytechnic over junior college

Had the grades for junior college and went the other way instead, to study marketing sooner. Got a fair number of "are you sure?" questions at the time.

2023 · NUS, year one

Joined a lot of student groups

Entrepreneurship society, the business club, a few others. In hindsight, probably too many at once.

2024 · Ho Chi Minh City

First internship abroad

Business development at a small startup, mostly building partnerships for a student programme instead of writing essays about how partnerships work.

2024 · Singapore

Co-founded a small startup

Built an AI wedding-planning tool with two friends, and got a small equity-free grant for it. It didn't go anywhere in the end, but it taught me more than most classes did.

2025 · New York

A year at a startup accelerator

Program associate work, mentor matching, communications, and watching a lot of startups try to enter the US market. Also ended up inside Nasdaq once, which still feels unlikely.

2025 · Columbia University

A hackathon I almost didn't sign up for

Thirty-six hours, not much sleep, about seventy competing teams. Our project placed second. It's one of the things I'm quietly proud of, without much of a story behind it.

2026 · Guangzhou

Brand marketing, on a different continent

A separate internship doing brand strategy for the China and Southeast Asia market, a fairly different world from startups.

Now · Singapore

Senior year, still deciding

Interning in marketing at a SaaS company while finishing my degree, and genuinely still unsure what comes after. Trying to treat that as ordinary, not a problem.

what I've come to think, most days

Not having it figured out isn't the same as being behind.

I used to think everyone else had a clear plan and I'd missed the memo. It turns out most people are also unsure. They've just gotten good at making updates sound more decisive than they feel.

If you're in the middle of your own version of this (internship rejections, choosing between "safe" and "interesting," or just not knowing what you want), I don't have advice that fixes it. But I'm happy to talk about it.
