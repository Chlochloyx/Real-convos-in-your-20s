Oct 3
growth

Grateful for the people I'm surrounded by, they inspire me so much

I've been thinking about how everyone around me seems to be doing so well lately, actually pursuing what they love. Some went overseas for full-time careers, so far away from home. Some started their own startups and are already going ahead to get funding. Some are just really good at what they do, thriving in the fields and roles they picked. Some are still building community, like a girls hangout group they run alongside their full-time jobs, which I think is so cool.

All of us are just striving to work hard for our own lives, in completely different directions, and honestly it's their grit, their passion, the way they dream that inspires me the most. I'm so thankful to be surrounded by people like this. It's great having them around me, it pushes me to actually go for what I want too.

family

Using my own money to actually give back

I did a full-time internship over the summer, and I'm interning again now, took the sem off to actually focus on it properly. It's such a good feeling, and more than anything it's made me want to use what I earn to give back to the people around me instead of just spending it on myself.

That's part of why I've been doing more small things for my family lately, went for a bracelet making class together, cooked for my sister's birthday, treated my parents to a meal. None of it needs to be anything fancy, just showing up for the people who raised me is enough.

Sep 19
quality time

Why quality time means so much to me

I've realized quality time is how I feel loved, and how I show love too. I'm a busy person, so when someone actually takes the time off their busy life to make time for me, I really value it, more than they probably realize.

And I try to do the same for the people who mean a lot to me. If someone matters to me, I make the effort to actually be there, to properly make time for them instead of just squeezing them in. That's just how I love people.

For me, if I'm there, I'm there, and I want the other person to actually know it too. So unless it's a workday and something's actually urgent, my phone goes in my bag, or just gets put flat down on the table.

A friend asked who my bridesmaids would be

A friend asked me the other day, kind of randomly, who my bridesmaids would be if I ever got married, and it turned into this brief little conversation about maid of honour and the whole bridal party. Funny how a random question like that can turn into actual reflection.

There's honestly a lot to think about, some elements of it, like who'd actually be good at handling something like a wedding emergency, or who could speak well enough to give a proper toast. I do have really close friends though, so it's still something I actually sit and think about, even from just a random conversation.

Sep 5
adulting

Graduating next year already, wait, so fast?

I realized the other day that I'm graduating next year and it hit me harder than I expected. Wait, so fast? It feels like just yesterday I was starting university, stressed about assignments and deadlines, and now suddenly there's an actual timeline counting down to when I have to properly step into the real world.

I don't think I'm ready, if I'm honest, and I don't think anyone really is. There's no such thing as being fully prepared to graduate and figure out the rest of your life, you just get there and deal with it as it comes. Still, it's a weird feeling watching the finish line get closer faster than I thought it would.

Aug 28
friendship

We're all going to end up leading our own lives

It's crazy how you drift away from friends as you grow older, not because anything went wrong, just because life takes you in different directions. Some people get married, some have kids, some move overseas for work, and slowly the group chat goes quiet, or you realize it's been months since you last properly caught up.

It's a strange thing to sit with, knowing that we're all eventually going to lead our own separate lives, even the friends who once knew everything about you. I don't think it means the friendship wasn't real. I just think growing up means everyone's timeline starts moving at a different pace, and you have to make peace with that instead of taking it personally.

Aug 20
career

So this is what working for the rest of my life looks like

Sometimes I sit with the thought of actually working 9 to 5 for the rest of my life and it freaks me out a little. Is this really it, waking up, working, coming home tired, doing it again the next day, for the next 40 odd years until retirement. Nobody really prepares you for how permanent that feels once you actually think about it properly.

I know not every job is a rigid 9 to 5, and I know people find real meaning in their work, careers they actually love. There's still this part of me that wonders if we're all just accepting this as the default because nobody ever really questions it out loud. I don't have an answer, I'm just thinking about it a lot lately.

I think it doesn't actually have to feel that way if I find something I love doing, where work stops feeling like a chore and starts being something I actually look forward to. A lot of that comes down to the people too, the colleagues you end up surrounded by every day. That's probably why company culture matters so much when you're picking a job, actually going on Glassdoor, asking around, not just going for the title or the pay.

Aug 14
relationships

What actually matters in a relationship

If there's one thing I've come to believe about relationships, it's that communication matters more than almost anything else. Someone who can actually communicate, who can sit down and talk things through instead of shutting down or avoiding it, matters so much more than someone who ticks every other box on paper.

It doesn't mean you'll never fight, every close relationship has disagreements somewhere along the way. What actually matters is how you come back together afterward, whether you can talk it out honestly instead of letting it fester, whether you're both comfortable going into the harder, deeper conversations instead of tiptoeing around them. That's the real test to me now, not whether you fight, it's how you resolve it.

Aug 8
growth

My biggest fear is looking back and realizing nothing changed

My biggest fear isn't even failing, it's looking back at myself in one year, three years, five years and realizing nothing actually changed, that I didn't really improve. It's good to have goals so we keep climbing, but the annoying thing about being human is we always strive for more, we're never just satisfied and staying there.

I really thought once I entered uni I'd finally feel happy and content. I got in, don't get me wrong it wasn't easy staying consistent throughout poly just to get here, but once I was here I just kept wanting more again. We always feel like we're never enough, that's just how it goes I think.

I think it's important to actually sit down and look back once in a while instead of only looking forward, and celebrate that, because that dream you always had, the one you don't even bring up anymore, you've probably already gone and achieved some version of it without even noticing.

Jul 30
family

Small habit, same one name every time

One small habit I've picked up that's actually been really great, every now and then I sit down and write out what I'm grateful for, doesn't matter where I am or what kind of week it's been. And honestly, most of the time it just comes down to one thing. My family.

No matter how far I am, how busy I get with work or school, whatever happens, they're always there. Doesn't matter how much I've messed up or how little I've checked in. And for that I'm genuinely grateful, more than I probably say out loud.

It's easy to get caught up in work and studies and forget the people around you. So this is just me reminding myself, don't forget to actually spend time with the people you love while you can. Cherish it.

Jul 10
new york

Do the thing that scares you, you'll never know what you'd have missed

Sometimes you just have to do the thing that scares you the most. You never know what you'll miss out on if you don't try, and I'd rather try than spend years regretting it. Of course there's so many unknowns, but you've just got to do what's best for you.

Going to New York for NOC was scary, I'd never been to the States before and this time it was alone, so far from home. Everyone around me was worried the whole time because of how it's portrayed in the media, like it's unsafe. And I'm not going to deny it, it can be unsafe, you do still have to be vigilant. But it's honestly one of the best decisions I've ever made.

What do you mean after 20 years of school, this is what you get

The economy is not the best right now and so many of my peers are struggling to even get a job. Sometimes at night I'll think, am I even in the right direction, should I have taken another degree, business is so general and so many people are struggling to get a job after grad, that worries me a lot.

Like what do you mean, after going through 20 years of education, this is what you get. I was crashing out too because I was trying to find an internship for summer, applied to so many, only got a few interviews, and then when I actually get into the interview round there's so many candidates to compete against. So stressful. It's just tough, makes me think how I'm even going to make a living in the future.
