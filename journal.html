growth

The fear of staying exactly the same

My biggest fear isn't failing, it's looking back at myself from one year, three years, five years ago and realizing that nothing actually changed. Goals are good, they're what keep you climbing, but the flip side of always striving for more is that you can forget to ever notice when you've arrived somewhere.

I used to think getting into uni would finally make me feel content. It didn't, not because uni disappointed me, but because the second I got here, I started wanting the next thing. Staying consistent through poly to get to this point wasn't easy, and I don't say that to brag, I say it because I barely let myself sit with it before moving the goalpost again. Maybe the fix isn't wanting less. Maybe it's just remembering to stop sometimes and notice that the thing you used to only dream about, you're already standing in it.

family

Three things, out loud

I try to write down three things I'm grateful for every so often, and almost every time, my family makes the list without me even having to think about it. No matter what's going on, how busy I've been, or how much I've messed up, they're just there.

It's easy to let that fade into the background when you're buried in work or school, to treat the people who show up for you as a given instead of something worth noticing. So this is mostly a reminder to myself: whatever's on your plate, don't forget to spend real time with the people you love. Cherish it while it's still easy to.

new york

Going to New York alone, scared the whole time

Sometimes you just have to do the thing that scares you the most, because you'll never know what you would've missed if you didn't try, and I'd rather try and be wrong than spend years wondering.

Going to New York for NOC was one of those. I'd never been to the US before, and this time I was going alone, further from home than I'd ever been. Everyone around me was worried, mostly because of how the US gets portrayed in the news, and I'm not going to pretend that worry came from nowhere, it's a real place with real risks, you do have to stay aware. But I went anyway, stayed vigilant, and it turned out to be one of the best decisions I've made.

Jun 20
career

20 years of school for this?

The economy isn't great right now, and a lot of my peers are struggling to even land a job. Some nights I lie there wondering if I picked the right path, if a business degree is too general, if I should have studied something else entirely. So many people are struggling to find work after graduating and it scares me. What do you mean after twenty years of education, this is what you get?

I felt that hard while hunting for a summer internship: applied to more places than I want to admit, only heard back from a handful, and then walked into interview rounds stacked with candidates all going for the same one spot. It's stressful in a way that's hard to explain unless you're in it, and it keeps circling back to the same question: how am I actually going to make a living after this?
